Excellent work that was started at McGill Faculty of Engineering by Samuel Dayo and completed at UCI Samueli School of Engineering by Amirtha Chandrasekaran.
**JETCAS Highlight Paper**: Near Real-Time Spike-Driven Synaptic Plasticity for CTT-Based Neuromorphic Systems

Authors: Amirtha Chandrasekaran, Samuel Dayo, Mousa Karimi, Boris Vaisband

Summary: Neuromorphic systems based on compute-in-memory (CIM) architectures offer a promising approach for energy-efficient edge artificial intelligence, but implementing biologically plausible online learning within non-volatile synaptic arrays remains a significant challenge. This work presents a spike-driven synaptic plasticity (SDSP) weight modification unit (WMU) for charge-trap transistor (CTT)-based neuromorphic systems, enabling near real-time in-situ synaptic weight adaptation within CTT-based CIM arrays. The proposed architecture partitions the learning functionality into neuron-specific and synapse-specific modules that evaluate post-synaptic activity, generate potentiation and depression events, and update synaptic weights while minimizing disruption to inference. Implemented in GF 22nm FDSOI CMOS technology, the WMU occupies 13.72 μm² per synapse and 85.63 μm² per neuron, with power dissipation of 0.14 μW per synapse and 19.61 μW per neuron. Compared with state-of-the-art neuromorphic learning architectures, the proposed design achieves 1.6×–17.9× area reduction for a neuron with 100 synapses while maintaining low-power operation and supporting scalable integration. This work establishes an efficient hardware framework for biologically plausible online learning in CTT-based CIM systems.
